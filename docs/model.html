The 4-Touch Autopilot · Central Command · 14 September 2026

One file. One line. Nobody waits.

This is the whole model in seven pictures. Every one of them is built and live. Read it top to bottom once and you will be able to explain it to Jess in five minutes.

01

Everything writes on the customer's file

There is one record per customer. Every text, email, note, document, ask, invoice and payment lands on it. The four companies, the four rooms and the customer all touch the same page. That is the whole trick: communication breaks when the answer lives somewhere else. Here it cannot.

02

The line: every customer runs down the same track

Each stage has one owning seat and one clock. When the stage's ask closes, the file moves to the next seat by itself. Fence and Oasis jobs run the top row. Roofing adds the milestone row inside Production. Nobody has to remember what comes next.

03

An ask closes on the input, never on a checkbox

This is the piece you liked in CC and the piece CC never enforced. An ask is a question the file needs answered. It sits on one seat with a clock. It only closes when the actual thing lands: the permit number, the photo, the date, the PDF. That input stays on the file forever, so nobody asks for it twice. Closing it opens the next ask on the next seat and texts the customer.

04

Nobody waits: the answer clock and the village

Your words: communication is the number one reason things break. No response, or a slow one. So every inbound text starts a clock on the seat that owns the customer right now. If it runs out, the stage's watcher sees red. And because the whole thread is on the file, anyone in the village can grab it and answer. Jess's team already works this way in the office. Now the whole company can.

05

Who sees what: three lanes, four rooms, one door

The file has three lanes. The customer's line is what they see. The office lane and the production lane are the team's. The database decides who reads which, not the page, so a rep on the phone app and Jess on a laptop get exactly their view of the same file.

SalesReps in the phone app. The hype thread. Jermey's desk. The console's sales section. They see their customers' line and the signing checklist.
OfficeJess's team. The queue of every ask the field is waiting on, oldest first. The workflow map. The switches. The office room.
ProductionSupervisors. Every sold customer, who holds them, for how long. Take the job, hand it back, sign off with photos. The production room.
The villageEverybody. One room for the whole company, plus the two department rooms. Post, react, and point a post at a customer's file.
06

What sits beside Contractors Cloud today

Nothing has been switched. The office still types into CC. The picture below is the whole plumbing: what flows in, what flows out, and where each switch sits. Solid lines run today. Dashed lines are built and waiting for your word.

07

How we migrate without a hiccup

The office runs three companies out of CC. They do not get a new system dropped on them. They get one job at a time, adopted with one tap the first time they open it, and the same steps they already know, shorter. Every step below can stop, and nothing breaks.

What shrinksA fence job is 10 CC tasks somebody creates and ticks. Here it is 4 office inputs: the paperwork, the permit number, the locate, the start date. Roofing goes from 21 tasks to 4 office inputs and 7 supervisor taps. Everything else opens itself, texts itself, and closes on the thing that arrived.
What staysThe names of the steps. Jess's wording on every text. CC's own lead-source list. The reps' hype thread. The phone app. Nobody learns a new vocabulary.
What goes away"Follow up with homeowner" as a task (6,217 of them, mostly dead). Week 1, 2 and 3 calls. The PM folder. Asking for the same paper twice. The Heymarket bill. The question "did anyone answer that?"
