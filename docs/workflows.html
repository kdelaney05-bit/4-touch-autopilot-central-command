04

The proposed model: the core efficiencies

One input per step, opened by the machineA fence job was 10 tasks somebody had to create, assign and tick. It becomes 5 office inputs, each one waiting in the queue the moment it is possible, each one closing on the actual thing. Roofing: 21 tasks become 6 office inputs and 7 supervisor taps.
The customer is told, every time, without anyone typingConfirmation, permit in, date set, invoice out, review ask. Five texts in Jess's words from the brand's main line, sent at the moment the ask closes. Today those are five Heymarket habits or five things that do not happen.
Nobody waits, nobody chasesEvery inbound text starts a clock on the seat that owns the customer. Red goes to the watcher. Anyone in the village can answer from the same line. "Follow up with homeowner" and the Week 1, 2, 3 calls, 6,725 tasks in CC, disappear.
Money closes itselfProposed: sign-off queues the QuickBooks invoice from the file; QuickBooks says paid, the payment ask closes, the review text goes, the job closes out. Laura reviews instead of retypes. Three steps leave the office entirely.
Production is on the file, not a whiteboardThe supervisor takes the job with one tap, walks the milestones with photos, signs off with the customer's word. The office sees every step without a call. Photos land on the file, so the invoice and the review have their proof.
One place, one vocabularyThe step names are CC's. The texts are Jess's. The lead sources are CC's list. The reps keep the phone app and the hype thread. What changes is that nobody has to remember what comes next, and nobody has to ask twice.

What stays human on purpose: the estimate, the paperwork uploads, the permit, the locate, the start date, the inspection result, the milestones, the sign-off. Those are judgment and paper. Everything between them is the machine's.
