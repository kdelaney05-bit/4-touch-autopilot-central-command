# The gospels

Kevin, 13 Sep 2026: "please write all these gospels in our creation. They work
everywhere." These are the rules every room, card, page and message in the
4-Touch Autopilot is built under. When a screen breaks one, the screen is
wrong, not the gospel.

## How a screen behaves

1. **Anyone can step in.** Someone who doesn't work in that department, Kevin
   included, can look in when the owner of the desk isn't there and know what
   to do, or at least which button keeps things moving. If a screen needs an
   expert to read it, it isn't finished.
2. **Important things are visually obvious, good or bad.** A red owner
   mismatch, a green accept, a clock past the line, money landed: it shows on
   the room's door before anyone opens a file. Nothing important hides in a
   detail view.
3. **The next step shouts.** A card doesn't hint at what happens next to
   someone who might know. It says NEXT, in one line, with the button beside
   it. Every file, every ask, every room. The next step is never nuance or
   intuition: it is automatic, or it is screaming at a human. Non-negotiable.
   And every human fails at some point, that is human nature, so anything we
   need a human for is on the list to automate next.
4. **The file is the hub.** Text, tag, document, send to, invoice, collect,
   estimate, property, paperwork: one customer file, every seat writes on it.
   Nothing lives in a second place.
5. **Count once per surface, and say the source.** A number on a screen comes
   from live rows the seat can read, is counted once, and says where it came
   from and when.
6. **Build beside, switch nothing.** New rails run next to Contractors Cloud
   with the switch OFF until Kevin flips it. Nothing is migrated by surprise.
7. **Every outbound word is a draft until Kevin says send.** Emails, texts,
   posts to the reps. Drafted, never sent, until the word.
8. **Signing is not selling.** A customer's ACCEPT records a yes and starts
   the paperwork. Money enters through the bell and Contractors Cloud, never
   from a signature.
9. **Never edit a published version in place.** Terms, forms, disclosures get
   a new version and a new pointer. What a customer saw stays what they saw.

## How the business behaves

10. **Revenue is the only thing that really matters.** Sales and marketing are
    the same thing and they are the job. Everything else is noise, and the
    noise is always louder. Look for more of the most important thing most of
    the time, especially the most important person in the company.
11. **Where it came from, how to get it, where to get more.** Ask it about
    every customer and every dollar.
12. **Effort = success.** The touch you almost didn't make is the one that
    gets the job. Receipts prove it.
13. **You are the product.** Sales is a survival skill, the most important one
    in the world; know the customer better than the trade, the trade you can
    learn.
14. **The 4-Touch, inside 72 hours, every appointment.** Show up. Thank you,
    no ask. Sneak back in. Ask before they decide.
15. **A no is as good as a yes.** A decided file clears the board and gives
    the time back.
16. **Marketing is knowing what it will cost and doing it for less than it
    costs anyone else.** A referral costs a text.
17. **Manage expectations from day one.** A new rep's first morning covers
    everything besides selling (the Tim lesson).
18. **Iterate forever.** Nothing is finished. Every screen, every touch,
    every form: make it better, faster, easier, then do it again.
19. **The phone is human. Always.** Sam, Jess and Laura answer the calls and
    set the appointments. That is nuance, not a task: thoughtful, pretty-
    sounding humans who are really good at engagement. The machine may dial,
    log, remind and confirm; it never books the appointment or answers the
    customer's call. And a human shows up for the appointment: five minutes
    early, in the collared shirt. The geofence only stamps it.
