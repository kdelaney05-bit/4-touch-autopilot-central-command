Behind the page

The signing page already exists. Sign-from-the-estimate has been live since August with the typed name, the drawn signature, the timestamp, the hashed IP, the device, and which terms were shown. Your own test signature from 12 Sep is in the table.

What gets added: a packet with several documents, one adopted signature applied per tap, a stamped PDF per document with an audit page, and the paperwork asks closing themselves when the stamped PDF lands on the file.

What each stamped PDF carries

The document as shownPage for page, with the signature image and initials placed where the form wants them.
The audit pageSigner's typed name, the time, a hashed IP, the device, the document's own hash, the terms version, and the consent text they tapped.
Where it landsThe customer's file, in the paperwork section, visible to the office the moment it exists. Sam never asks "did they sign?".
03

The button Sam needs: Turn in the job

On the rep's file. Red until every piece is there. Nothing moves to the office until it's green, and the rep sees exactly which piece is holding it. If something is still missing 24 hours after signing, the rep gets an ask with a clock and a push, and Sam's queue reads "waiting on Travis" instead of her chasing him.

The owner line is the one that catches people. The county is asked the moment the customer accepts, and every roll runs behind — nightly at best, months on the statewide fallback — so a customer who closed three weeks ago signs while the appraiser still prints the people he bought it from. When the name does not match, the piece is the warranty deed out of his closing package, and it is the rep's, on the spot: the estimate screen says so before the signature, because a photo taken in the driveway costs nothing and a week of chasing costs the permit. The NOC is sworn by the owner, so nothing prints until it lands. The whole rule.

Only when the file needs a wet-ink or notarized form. The customer can pick a slot, but only inside the next two days. Anything further out goes through the office. The rep gets the visit as an ask with a clock, so a slow-played homeowner can't stall the permit.
