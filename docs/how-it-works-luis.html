0 · The one thing to know

The notification is the instruction. Nobody has to think.

You never go looking for what to do next. When something needs you, it comes to you, with your name on it, and it says what to do and gives you the button. That is the whole system, and it is the same for Sam, Jonathan, Laura, Obed, Robert, Mike and the crews.

How it reaches youThree ways, all at once: the bing and a card on the desk, the buzz on your phone, and an email with a link straight to that customer's file. Nothing reaches you unless your name is on it. No feed to read, no list to check.
What it saysWho, which customer, what to do, and by when. One line. "Obed signed off, 6 photos on the file, invoice when you can." "Material is short at Marchetti, 2 posts, see the photo." "Sold and waiting for a supervisor: Reed, Dana, Melbourne."
What you pressEvery one ends in a button: Got it, Open the file, or Done with the proof. You press it and the next step opens on the next person by itself. You never decide the order; the last step opens the next one.
If nothing cameThen nothing is on you. That is the rule. Not "check just in case". When a job is quiet, it is quiet.

If you ever have to think about what to do next, that is a bug. Tell Kevin which screen, and he fixes the screen, not you.

3 · The file is the hub

One customer, one file, every seat writes on it.

Open any customer and you see the whole story in order: their texts and ours, the emails, the team's notes, every step the office took, every photo, every document. Who moved through it, in colour. And the line that matters:

NEXT one sentence, the loudest thing on the file: what happens next and who it is waiting on. Automatic, or a named human. Never a guess.

The buttons across the top:

CallTextTagEstimate+ DocumentSend to…InvoiceCollectTake the jobHand it back
  • Text goes to the customer from the company's main line, credited to you. Six seconds to undo. The pre-written lines on the right fill the box; edit, then Send.
  • Tag is a note to the team the customer never sees. @Obed, @Sam, @supers, @office: they get the bing on the desk, the buzz on the phone, and an email with the link to this file.
  • Send to… hands the file to a seat with a note. Take the job puts a sold job on you. Hand it back sends it back with why.
  • + Document puts a permit, an HOA letter, anything, on the file. Estimate builds the itemized estimate the customer taps ACCEPT on.

Three lanes on every file. CHAT is the homeowner's lane, write there like they are reading it. SUPER is you and the crew. OFFICE is staff only; the homeowner can never reach it, the database makes sure.

Asks with a clock. Anything someone has to do is an ask with a name on it and a clock running: the permit, the locate, the material, the install date, your sign-off, the invoice, the payment. Done closes it with the proof (a number, a photo, a date), and the next one opens by itself.

4 · The chain

How a job moves, and where you come in.

Signed›Paperwork · Sam›Permit›811 locate · Sam›Material · Jonathan›Install date · Jonathan›Crew · you›Invoice · Laura›Payment›Done

Nobody decides the next step; the last one opens it. The customer signs, the office's checklist opens on Sam. The last paper in is paperwork official, and the material opens on Jonathan with the order attached. The date is set, and it lands on you.

What lands on you, and when:

  • The job, sold and waiting. Production room, "sold and waiting for a supervisor". Take it, or it is assigned to you with a note.
  • The work order. The moment the install date is set, the file writes the work order and emails it to you and to the crew, Spanish and English, with the drawing and the material list. The crew taps RECIBIDO with a name; you see who received it and when.
  • From the field, on your file: a material run, a site issue, a safety photo, a change order, a ping. Each one is an ask on you with the clock running.
  • Sign-off. When the work is done: three or more finished photos and your word. That one tap is the trigger for everything after it.
NEXT Your sign-off opens INVOICE READY on the office by itself: the signed amount, the deposit, the change orders, your photos, your name, all typed. Laura checks it and taps Approve. You never call about an invoice again.
5 · Your crews

Today: the nugget and the work order. Next: one login per crew.

Today, no login. On The Line, My crews: a crew is a name, a phone and a language. Send a nugget: one instruction, the customer and the address, the picture with the scope and the price, and the one thing to bring back (a photo, a number, sí/no, done). They get a text with a link, in Spanish. You hold the receipt: texted, opened, RECIBIDO by name, brought back. Nothing to argue about. Open any photo on a file, Send to a crew, and the nugget is already filled.

The work order is the same page for the whole job: the order, the drawing, four big buttons (AQUÍ · FOTOS · LISTO · LLAMAR) and a line to you. Every tap lands on the customer's file with the crew's name.

The text with the link. The moment the install date is set, the crew lead's phone gets a text from the company line, in both languages: the customer, the address, the date, and the link. The page opens in the phone's own language, with two big buttons, ESPAÑOL and ENGLISH, always in view. Nobody is assumed to read Spanish, or English. That link is the room for that job: the crew, you, the supervisor. Everything the crew taps or writes lands on the customer's file in your lane with the crew's name, the photos included, and you get the push. What you write on the file in that lane shows up on their link. No app, no login, no password for a crew, ever. Today the link goes by email only; the text is one switch Kevin flips (The crew's text, in the Office room). It needs a phone on the crew's card, which is why the roster matters.

Next COMING: a text back to the crew when you answer, so they never have to reopen the link to see it; and the translation both ways, his words kept beside it.

9 · When something goes wrong

Send it to Kevin. Then keep working the old way.

  1. Reply to Kevin's email, the one this came with. That thread is the ticket. One line is enough: which screen, the exact words on it, and a screenshot if you can. Do not start a new email; do not let it wait for a meeting.
  2. If a crew is standing there waiting, text or call Kevin on his cell right then. The email can follow.
  3. Meanwhile, nothing stops. Contractors Cloud, the phones, the crews' texts: all of it runs exactly as before. Nothing has been switched off. Do it the old way for that one job and say so on the file.
  4. The three fixes that solve most of it: on the site, tap Refresh at the top, or sign out and back in. In the app, close it all the way and reopen it. On the phone, one wifi bar is worse than none; turn wifi off and try again.
  5. If the app says "Could not reach the live database" or anything like it, that is a known one: sign out and in once, and if it says it again, send Kevin those exact words and the time. He needs the words, not "it didn't work".
  6. If a crew hits something (the link will not open, the photo will not send): they tell you, you send it to Kevin the same way. Crews never email Kevin; you are their line.

Nothing you do on a screen can break a job. The worst case is a screen that is wrong, and that gets fixed the same day.
